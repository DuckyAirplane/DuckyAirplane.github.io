Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout the Computer Science program, I have become more confident working through technical problems and building software from an idea into something functional. My coursework gave me experience with programming, databases, security, algorithms, full-stack development, and testing while also teaching me how to research problems when the first solution does not work. I have also learned that computer science involves more than writing code because communication, planning, feedback, and understanding what a user actually needs can affect the success of a project. These experiences have helped prepare me for a career in computer science and have strengthened my interest in software and game development.

My coursework also helped me build skills that are important when working with other developers and stakeholders. I learned to organize code so it can be understood and maintained by someone else, document my decisions, follow project requirements, and use feedback to improve my work. Those skills are important in a team environment because software development depends on people being able to understand each other’s work and make decisions based on shared goals.

My technical skills grew across several areas of computer science. I worked with data structures and algorithms to think through how a program handles information and solves problems, while software engineering courses gave me experience designing, testing, and improving larger applications. Database work taught me how applications store, retrieve, and protect data, and my security coursework made me more aware of vulnerabilities, authentication, validation, and the need to consider security throughout development instead of adding it at the end.

Other coursework gave me experience beyond the artifacts in this portfolio. In my security work with Artemis Financial, I evaluated software vulnerabilities, used testing tools to identify weaknesses, and considered ways to reduce security risks in an application. Projects like that helped me see security as part of the development process and gave me experience looking at software from both the developer's side and the side of someone trying to find weaknesses in it.

The artifacts in this portfolio bring those skills together through three different areas of computer science. Travlr Getaways demonstrates my work in software engineering and databases, while the Deep Q-Learning Maze Agent demonstrates my experience with algorithms and artificial intelligence. Together, the code review, enhancements, and supporting narratives document how I improved existing work, solved problems that came up during development, and applied skills from across the Computer Science program.
